Starting a live music festival in November 2020 was, on paper, a bad idea. The
constraint turned out to be the useful part: every edition had to be planned
around rules that could change in a week, which forced a kind of operational
discipline that a normal event would never have demanded.

Five editions later, the more interesting problem became succession. A festival
that only works because one person is holding it together is not a festival, it
is a hobby. Moving into a mentoring role — and having the team run it well
without me — is the outcome I am actually proud of.
